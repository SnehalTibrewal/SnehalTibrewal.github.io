Research

In a compact binary system, objects like black holes or neutron stars can continue dancing around each other for millions of years. With passing time, they keep getting closer while emitting energy in the form of gravitational waves, until they finally collide. These waves encode everything there is to know about these objects, and that's exactly what I’m interested in! I use tools like Bayesian inference and numerical relativity to help disentangle the binaries' properties. In the last couple of years I have been intrigued by a particular property-- eccentricity. Its a measure of how non-circular a binary's orbit is and can often be telling about the birth story of that particular binary. Here I summarise some science I have done to better understand this orbital effect.

Misinterpreting spin precession as orbital eccentricity in gravitational-wave signals

S. Tibrewal, A. Zimmerman, J. Lange, D. Shoemaker
Phys. Rev. D 114, 064046 (2026)

Massive black-hole signals are often limited to a few observable cycles. With limited data, it can be hard to tell which physical effects were part of the underlying dynamics of the binary. In this project I explored whether a precessing binary i.e binary with a wobbling orbital plane, might be mistaken for an eccentric binary. We tested this by first computing broadstroke mismatches between precessing and eccentric systems across a large parameter space, followed by zooming in on the best matched precessing systems. We found that one particular precessing, non-eccentric system out of all we studied was indeed misinterpreted as having measurable eccentricity. That established the first definite example of a system where this degeneracy has been obsesrved. However, the singular finding pointed to a bigger takeaway-- this degeneracy is a highly localized effect and isn't universal for all short signals. A fine tuned combination of intrinsic parameters as well as the viewing angle may be required to observe this effect.

Impact of eccentricity and higher modes on neutron star–black hole parameter estimation

S. Tibrewal, A. Jan, A. Zimmerman, H.-Y. Chen
arXiv:2607.16135 (2026)

Astrophysically, most neutron star–black hole systems are expected to retain little to no eccentricity in the LVK sensitivity band. However, GW200105, an NSBH, is supected to have significant(e>0.05) measurable eccentricity. In this project, we asked what additional information could we extract from an eccentric NSBH. We systematically studied systems modeled after GW200105 and investigated the effect of changing eccentricity on the binary parameters as well as the higher order mode content. We found that eccentricity dramatically sharpens the mass and spin measurements by more than tenfold. This points to eccentricity not just being an additional source parameter, but also an important property to better contraint other source properties of the binary.
